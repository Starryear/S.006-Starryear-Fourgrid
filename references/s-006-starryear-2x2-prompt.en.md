# 【S-006】Starryear-2x2 Full Prompt

Treat one user-uploaded photograph as the sole source of content, composition, color, and emotion. Create one precise vertical 2:3 four-panel abstract editorial artwork; do not produce four filters, four unrelated images, miniature scene reconstructions, or generic ink decoration.

## 1. Input roles and priority

**Image 1 = the user's source photograph.** Image 1 alone determines subject matter, visual soul, dominant energy, hue relationships, warm/cool balance, saturation, luminance, contrast, tonal hierarchy, atmosphere, spatial relationships, masses, axes, directions, count groups, depth, overlaps, rhythm, and emotional temperature.

If an **Image 2 = abstraction-method reference** is supplied, use it only to understand progression from representation to abstraction; transformation of objects into lines, fields, marks, modules, and rhythms; structural axes; repetition; overlaps; scale relationships; and full-field organization. Never borrow or approximate its palette, background, brightness, contrast, saturation, texture, subject, copy, title, exact composition, shapes, or mark placement.

If any secondary rule conflicts with “Image 1 is the sole source of content and color,” that source-lock rule has highest priority.

## 2. Internal visual analysis

Complete these judgments internally and do not output the analysis: What force dominates? What is fixed and temporary? Where does movement begin and where does it travel? What encloses or interrupts the scene? Where are the strongest contrast and emotional anchor? What repeats? Where do intervals expand or contract? What is dense or open? Which relationship would remain if realistic detail disappeared?

Reduce the answer to one short `SOUL STATEMENT`, such as “FIXED MASS / PASSING RHYTHM” or “DARK ENCLOSURE / DISTANT LIGHT.” These examples show form only; do not copy them automatically. Panels 2–4 must express the same statement at different abstraction levels.

Also build an internal translation map: `source object → position and scale → directional trajectory → repeated count → interval and pause → density change → field pressure`. Panel 2 may retain very few object fragments. Panel 3 must discard the object silhouette and keep only verifiable trajectories, counts, intervals, and density. Panel 4 enlarges those relationships into collision, compression, expansion, or release. Judge abstraction by objects receding and relationships taking control, not merely by larger brushstrokes.

## 3. Color lock

Extract from Image 1: one dominant atmospheric color, one dark structural color, one light source color, one secondary supporting color, and the smallest important accent. Preserve their original temperature, luminance, saturation, contrast, and area relationships.

Every visible color in Panels 2–4, including their backgrounds, must be sampled or visually derived from Image 1. Their backgrounds should first inherit the source's largest atmospheric color. Use a pale or low-information source color only when the photograph itself is dominated by that kind of field. Local intensity may be reduced, but the overall hue bias, dominant-color area ratio, and luminance hierarchy must still come from Image 1.

Keep cool sources cool, warm sources warm, and near-monochrome sources near-monochrome. A small saturated source color must remain a small accent. If a photograph is governed by a broad blue sky, blue water, dark enclosure, warm wall, or another strong dominant field, the generated panels must retain enough area and intensity of that color to remain immediately related; do not dilute it into pale paper or gray wash. If the source contains no gold, beige, green, or warm ivory, add none. Never default to `#F3F0E8`, ivory, cream, beige, vintage paper, Japanese neutrals, gray-green, global desaturation, or automatic warm toning.

## 4. Canvas and grid

Create one vertical 2:3 master canvas, recommended at 2000×3000 px, divided into a precise 2×2 grid of equal cells. Each cell must itself be vertical 2:3, recommended at 1000×1500 px. Use only very thin, clean divisions; add no outer frame, cards, or shadows.

Every generated cell must be a complete composition. Activate upper, middle, lower, left, center, right, edges, and corners. Let large forms enter and leave the frame, make principal lines cross substantial distances, distribute repeated marks across zones, and use large, medium, and small scales. Roughly 65–85% of each cell should participate through fields, marks, silhouettes, rhythms, or structurally shaped negative space. Breathing room is allowed; accidental dead corners are not.

## 5. Panel 1 — top left: PHOTOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE

Rebuild the top-left cell from the current Image 1 on every run and place its actual pixels faithfully. Never call, reuse, or imitate a previous test image, example image, or earlier generated result. Preserve the current source's original orientation, scene, exposure, color balance, contrast, space, overlaps, and photographic character whenever possible. Permit only proportional scaling, restrained crop, or source-colored padding needed to fit vertical 2:3. If the generator cannot guarantee pixel fidelity, generate the other cells first and deterministically composite the current source back over Panel 1.

Do not redraw, regenerate, recolor, grade, stylize, outpaint, retouch, simplify, filter, or place typography over this panel.

## 6. Panel 2 — top right: POETIC DISTILLATION

Create a strongly abstracted, faintly recognizable poetic memory. Do not rebuild the photograph as a simplified scene or preserve complete objects, realistic silhouettes, natural perspective, or descriptive detail. Recognition may arise only indirectly through dominant direction, spatial pressure, light-dark hierarchy, asymmetry, repeated rhythm, major color relationships, and one or two incomplete identity cues.

Include one substantial source-colored atmospheric field, one large abstract mass entering or leaving an edge, one directional axis crossing most of the cell, one source rhythm distributed across multiple zones, several broken or dissolved source traces, and one small concentrated source accent.

Use an organic, wet, broken, dissolving language: overlapping fields, cropped edge masses, broken directional lines, translucent washes, bleeding edges, interrupted contours, and dissolved forms. Aim for 20–30% recognizable evidence and 70–80% abstract field, rhythm, and atmosphere. Retain only one or two incomplete cues; they must not combine into a readable miniature scene.

Do not use rational notation, coordinate grids, rows of solid nodes, precise short bars, or engineering-like axes in this panel. It must feel like a damp, drifting memory with disappearing edges, completely unlike Panel 3's dry structural language.

Add no title, subtitle, archive number, or other typography. Treat any calm area that might otherwise hold type as compositional negative space shaped by fields, edge tension, and directional relationships.

## 7. Panel 3 — bottom left: RHYTHMIC STRUCTURE

Turn the photograph into a full-cell point-line visual score whose parts can still be mapped back to the source. It must no longer depict the scene literally, but it must preserve the dominant axis, principal branch or subject trajectory, decisive intersections, landmark placement, count groups, intervals, clusters, pauses, density changes, reflections, depth, movement, and asymmetry. Translate them strictly into many unequal solid points, long hairlines, short line segments, intervals, count groups, and a few geometric breaks. Points and lines must carry most of the panel's information, rhythm, and spatial movement; keep pale flat bands rare and subordinate.

The upper zone may carry lighter distant rhythms and suspended dots; the middle zone must contain the main axis, strongest rhythm, and densest event; the lower zone may contain reflection echoes, compressed repetitions, secondary bands, or a long grounding line. Connect the zones into one continuous score.

Use points and lines as the two primary mark families and allow only one extremely weak supporting form. Establish at least three directional trajectories traceable to the source, with multiple node groups of unequal size, spacing, and density; nodes may connect, offset, suspend, or pause. Retain at least one highly reduced but traceable identity cue, such as a moon position, tower cadence, principal branch bend, human count group, or horizon reflection. Node counts, spacing, and size changes must map to observable buds, figures, windows, lights, branch forks, or other repeated source units; never arrange them as a generic Cartesian grid. Preserve unequal, breathing, interrupted, directional rhythm. Avoid business infographics, charts, perfect spacing, and decorative patterns. Dark lines organize structure, source-accent nodes mark beats, and pale values provide only hierarchy and pauses.

Strictly prohibit flower-like radial strokes, wet watercolor blossoms, splatter, dissolved object silhouettes, broad wet washes, and heavy expressive brushwork in this panel. Restrained low-contrast translucent source-color bands, broken brush residues, or dry/wet boundary traces may sit behind the notation so the cell shares material DNA with Panels 2 and 4; they may organize hierarchy but never overpower the point-line score. Apart from one minimal landmark cue, no complete organic object may remain. The panel must stay predominantly dry, thin, restrained, flat, and clear, with a stronger, denser, more continuous point-line pulse—like a private score extracted from the photograph rather than an isolated infographic.

Do not retain a complete source-object silhouette that reads at first glance. If the panel is still read first as a tree, flower, person, building, animal, vehicle, or other source object rather than as a score of points, lines, nodes, pauses, and count groups, the translation has failed. Identity may remain only indirectly through dominant trajectory, decisive intersection, count, and density.

## 8. Panel 4 — bottom right: SURREAL RELATIONAL AFTERWORLD

Select the strongest emotional-spatial relationship in the `SOUL STATEMENT` and rebuild it as an immersive surreal field with depth. Apply one fixed translation formula: `preserve the relationship map + replace object identity`.

First lock the source relationship map: relative positions of principal objects or masses, depth order, scale progression, overlaps, count groups, density gradient, dominant direction, enclosure, light-dark pressure, and visual anchor. The map must make the result feel born from the photograph without requiring the source objects' silhouettes.

Then invent a new set of spatial substitutes for this photograph. Translate source objects into nonliteral volumes, chambers, folds, portals, shells, terrains, suspended bodies, seams, passages, or other spatial units derived from the current source. These words describe functions, not a fixed asset library. A plant need not become a shell, a person need not become a statue, and architecture need not become a portal. Re-invent the substitutes for every source. Generated forms must not retain a complete outline, parts assembly, or surface detail that immediately identifies the original object class.

Include one source-colored environment occupying about 45–70% of the cell; one principal path, seam, or pressure direction crossing roughly 60–100% of its width or height; one large cropped volume entering from an edge; several medium spatial units; smaller echoes that recede or disperse; and one concentrated source accent. Build depth through occlusion, scale recession, internal light-dark modeling, translucent overlap, or structural seams so the result feels like an impossible place rebuilt from photographic memory rather than flat decoration.

Major elements may cross, collide, suspend, nest, overlap, compress, expand, divide the cell, or disappear beyond an edge. Preserve the source's visual weight, direction, temperature, dominant contrast, asymmetry, density, and emotional tension while replacing object identity.

Create estrangement through “the relationships remain, the objects have changed.” The panel fails if it reads first as a painted, illustrated, or filtered version of the source. It also fails if the source hierarchy, rhythm, direction, and pressure disappear into arbitrary geometry or decoration. The correct result sits between those failures: the source's spatial grammar remains traceable while its objects enter another state of being.

Prioritize opaque or translucent volume modeling, controlled internal luminance, and structural seams with selective hard-soft edges. Restrained mineral, gouache, matte screen-print, or thin sculptural surfaces are allowed, but no material is a default. Prohibit watercolor splatter, full-cell wet brushing, chaotic impasto, hard-edged wedge collage, corporate geometry, random circles, and generic futuristic architecture.

## 9. Shared visual DNA and surface

Panels 2–4 share one palette, principal direction, light-dark hierarchy, visual anchor, count group, asymmetry, rhythm, and `SOUL STATEMENT`. Transform the same source element progressively: a branch can move from incomplete cue to directional line system to a sweeping structural force; water can move from reflected field to horizontal echoes to a flowing major field.

The three generated cells must share perceptible material kinship while keeping fixed dominant-language separation. Panel 2 is light, wet, translucent, layered, fragmentary, and partially erased. Panel 3 is dry, thin, flat, and governed by points, lines, nodes, pauses, and count groups. Panel 4 is more opaque, dimensional, spatial, and inhabitable, governed by volumes, chambers, occlusion, scale recession, and structural seams. The result fails if Panels 2 and 4 both read first as similar wet brushwork, pale blobs, or the same edge system.

For every new photograph, rebuild the `SOUL STATEMENT`, palette, axes, count groups, landmark, crop, relationship map, and spatial substitutes from zero. Never hard-code tested-image subjects such as flowers, trees, water, buildings, or people, nor white-green palettes, hollow shells, glowing chambers, colors, counts, directions, positions, or shapes. Example works demonstrate only the four-stage transformation logic; they must never supply elements, palette, composition, or form templates. Panel 1 must never retain pixels from an example image.

The visual language may combine long ink-like structural lines, broken horizontal strokes, flat or lightly organic fields, partial arcs, irregular dots, short bars, restrained silhouettes, reflection echoes, and cropped edge masses, but do not use all types equally. Texture may occur only inside source-derived marks. Exclude random paper grain, stains, heavy aging, obvious canvas texture, digital glow, dramatic shadows, strong gradients, vignettes, and compression artifacts.
