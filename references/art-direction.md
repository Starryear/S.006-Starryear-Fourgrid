# Art direction

Use this reference after source analysis and before generating Panels 2–4. It defines the visual grammar; the photograph supplies every specific decision.

## 1. Core proposition

The quartet is a compact visual essay about one photographed scene. Each panel moves farther from literal appearance while preserving the same subject contour, structural rhythm, palette, direction, and emotional tension. The central junction is not a separator: it is the point where the subject changes material and passes between states.

Balance these tensions:

- evidence / imagination;
- recognizable / nonliteral;
- hard contour / soft field;
- stillness / directional movement;
- dense anchor / clean open space;
- photographic depth / paper-flat abstraction;
- real material / one restrained impossibility.

Do not resolve every panel toward the same side of a tension.

## 2. Layout and visual circulation

Use the fixed diagonal progression:

```text
┌─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┐
│ 1. EVIDENCE         │ 2. DISTILLATION     │
│ literal / detailed  │ remembered / surreal│
├────────── subject-line junction ──────────┤
│ 3. STRUCTURE        │ 4. ABSTRACT SOUL    │
│ rhythmic / analytic │ immersive / released│
└─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┘
```

The eye should circulate diagonally from the factual upper-left anchor to the expressive lower-right field, then return through the central subject-line collage. Balance dark and light diagonally so one side does not become heavy.

Use hairline seams or quiet value changes. The junction motif may interrupt the seam locally, but the grid must remain instantly legible.

## 3. Source transformation ladder

Choose three to five source anchors. Transform them with increasing distance:

| Source evidence | Panel 2 | Panel 3 | Panel 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| branch or body curve | reduced contour | directional line system | sweeping structural force |
| water or reflection | translucent echo | broken intervals | expanded flowing field |
| architecture | silhouette planes | axes and modules | monumental pressure or fold |
| repeated people, birds, windows, petals | partial cues | counted marks | dispersed tension nodes |
| horizon, path, shoreline | quiet division | principal axis | spatial cut or release |

Use only mappings the source supports. Preserve actual count when count contributes to identity.

## 4. Panel 1 — Photographic Evidence

Top left. Use the source photograph directly.

Preserve:

- primary subject identity and protected evidence;
- original orientation and viewpoint;
- light direction, exposure, and contrast;
- source color balance and saturation relationships;
- depth, overlap, occlusion, and background accidents;
- meaningful count and spacing.

Crop only to fit the 2:3 cell and protect the declared anchor. Do not redraw, regenerate, retouch, stylize, recolor, extend, simplify, or place text over it.

## 5. Panel 2 — Poetic Distillation

Top right. Recognition should survive through visual DNA, not surface detail.

Required structure:

- one broad atmospheric field derived from the source light or dominant color;
- one major line based on the principal subject contour;
- one observed repeated rhythm;
- one or two reduced recognition anchors;
- one small accent event;
- one subtle appearance of the controlled surreal behavior;
- one contour aimed toward the bottom-left corner and central junction.

Possible translations:

- reflective ground → translucent strokes or broken echoes;
- foliage → large cropped masses with actual source hues;
- architecture → simplified planes with preserved perspective pressure;
- small moving subjects → irregular marks following actual movement;
- sky, mist, snow, wall, or open water → one clean low-information field.

Keep one focal edge crisp. Use diffusion around it sparingly. The panel should feel like memory becoming material, not a watercolor copy.

## 6. Panel 3 — Rhythmic Structure

Bottom left. Reveal the source's structural heartbeat rather than its objects.

Extract:

- principal axis and major divisions;
- count groups and interval changes;
- clusters, pauses, and density transitions;
- perspective compression or expansion;
- reflection, echo, and directional movement;
- source asymmetry.

Choose one primary family from long lines, irregular bars, broken bands, partial arcs, or repeated modules. Add no more than two support families such as nodes, short dashes, faint echoes, or compressed marks.

- Upper zone receives the line from the central junction.
- Middle zone holds the main structural axis and densest event.
- Lower zone carries grounding, reflection, or compressed repetition.

Connect the zones into one breathing, interrupted score. Avoid chart-like cleanliness, perfect grids, equal spacing, and decorative pattern repetition.

## 7. Panel 4 — Abstract Soul

Bottom right. Embody the deepest force in the `SOUL STATEMENT`.

Required structure:

- one source-colored field occupying roughly 40–65% of the panel;
- one principal line, arc, or path crossing roughly 60–100% of its width or height;
- one large cropped mass entering from an edge;
- two to five medium structural forms;
- a few rhythmic traces rather than decorative filler;
- one concentrated source accent;
- one crisp identity contour;
- the clearest manifestation of the controlled surreal event.

Begin one relationship near the upper-left corner so it answers the central junction. Let forms cross, overlap, collide, divide, compress, expand, or disappear beyond edges. The result may be impossible, but its weight, direction, scale hierarchy, and palette must remain faithful.

## 8. Central junction collage grammar

The junction combines a real or materially faithful subject fragment with its distilled line. It should look as though the photograph is shedding a contour that becomes the structural score and then releases into ink.

Use two to four components only:

1. a small source crop, incomplete silhouette, or recognizable void;
2. one principal contour at high edge clarity;
3. one broken or repeated echo of that contour;
4. optionally one tiny accent fragment.

Allow fragments to overlap seams, but preserve transparent breathing space. Prefer asymmetrical extension into two or three quadrants rather than a perfectly centered radial emblem. The line must match the subject: branch growth stays botanical, architecture stays perspectival, a figure follows gesture, an animal follows anatomy, water follows reflection or flow.

Reject the junction when it reads as arbitrary flourish, stock brush mark, ornamental flower, sticker, badge, seal, paper scrap, portal, compass, or target.

## 9. Material and edge hierarchy

Use one dominant visual language across generated panels and vary its state:

- Panel 2: clean contour plus translucent memory field;
- Panel 3: dry precise structure plus limited print loss;
- Panel 4: broader chromatic ink field plus one crisp contour;
- junction: source fragment becoming clean contour and sparse rhythm.

Keep materials physically plausible but restrained. Suitable details include dry-brush drag, capillary tide line, diluted pigment, sparse halftone loss, print misregistration, or graphite residue. Use them locally, never as a universal dirt layer.

At least one edge in each generated panel must be deliberately sharp. Softness should have a reason—distance, wash, reflection, or material migration—and must not become uniform haze.

## 10. Minimalism without emptiness

Use fewer, larger, better-related forms. Every mark must answer at least one source fact: identity, line, count, direction, density, color role, overlap, or emotional tension.

Keep large clean intervals, but activate them through nearby pressure or direction. A quiet region is valid; an abandoned corner is not. Prefer one strong line over many weak strokes, two broad fields over mottled paint, and one exact accent over scattered color.

## 11. Forbidden shortcuts

- four different filters or color grades;
- redrawing the complete scene four times;
- a generic beige “Japanese editorial” preset;
- generic ink mountains, bamboo, cranes, moons, temples, seals, or calligraphy;
- invented surreal objects unrelated to the source;
- dense splatter, distressed paper, dirty overlays, muddy watercolor, or black wash dominance;
- perfectly symmetric geometry or infographics;
- isolated central logo, pasted card, heavy frame, drop shadow, or fake depth;
- multiple competing textures, fonts, or saturated accents;
- low-resolution blur, broken contours, malformed architecture/anatomy, or compression artifacts.
