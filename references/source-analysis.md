# Source-analysis sheet

Complete this privately before generating. Every answer must be based on visible evidence in the user's photograph.

## A. Photographic anchors

1. **Primary subject** — the person, animal, plant, architecture, landscape mass, object, or spatial relationship that makes the photograph specific.
2. **Identity contour** — the smallest silhouette or contour fragment that still identifies the primary subject.
3. **Protected evidence** — the face, gesture, building geometry, count, reflection, or overlap that Panel 1 and the central bridge must not obscure.
4. **Secondary anchors** — two to four source-specific details that may recur in reduced form.
5. **Viewpoint and depth** — camera angle, near/middle/far arrangement, occlusion, horizon, and perspective convergence.

## B. Structural evidence

1. **Dominant axis** — horizontal, vertical, diagonal, arc, spiral, radial, enclosing, or dispersed.
2. **Principal subject line** — the most characteristic edge, gesture, branch, roofline, shoreline, body curve, path, reflection, or shadow boundary.
3. **Count and rhythm** — repeated subjects or intervals; record the actual count when meaningful.
4. **Density map** — dense zone, open zone, pause, and transition.
5. **Movement** — where it begins, which direction it travels, and where it exits.
6. **Negative-space shape** — the largest meaningful sky, water, wall, shadow, gap, or bokeh field.

## C. Color and light lock

Record five roles without importing fashionable colors:

- dominant atmospheric color;
- darkest meaningful structural color;
- brightest meaningful source color;
- quieter secondary color;
- smallest important accent color.

Also record warm/cool balance, light direction, brightest region, darkest region, contrast level, and whether the accent occupies less than roughly 10% of the source. Preserve these roles in generated panels.

## D. Interpretive statement

Write one short `SOUL STATEMENT` as a tension between two observed forces, for example `FIXED MASS / PASSING LIGHT`. Examples define the syntax only; never select one unless it accurately describes the photograph.

Choose one `CONTROLLED SURREAL EVENT` that changes the behavior of existing evidence without adding a fantasy object. Suitable operations include continuation, scale shift, detached reflection, folded perspective, material migration, repeated echo, weightlessness, or shadow separation.

The event must satisfy all of these:

- traceable to the primary subject or principal subject line;
- visible mainly in Panels 2 and 4 and echoed structurally in Panel 3;
- legible without explaining it;
- subordinate to the photograph rather than becoming a new story;
- singular: do not combine multiple surreal tricks.

## E. Central junction plan

Define one `JUNCTION MOTIF` for the exact central cross of the 2×2 grid:

- subject fragment: one small source-derived crop, silhouette, void, or material fragment;
- subject line: one clean traced or interpreted principal contour;
- direction: the two or three quadrants it should visually connect;
- protected area: the source anchor it must avoid;
- palette: the source dark structural color plus at most one source accent;
- coverage: normally 3–8% of the total master area.

The junction motif must behave like a dimensional hinge: photographic evidence becomes line, line becomes rhythm, and rhythm opens into the abstract soul. It must not look like a sticker, centered badge, decorative flower, tape scrap, circular logo, or fifth panel.
